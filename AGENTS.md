Working notes for anyone building on this project.

## History

development on 2026-11-15. The editor is disconnected: nothing in the repo
depends on it, and the build runs on the plain Vite config in

## Cards and manufacturing (product rule, user, 2026-11-15)

Card manufacturing is outsourced end to end. Renda's job is the batch: build it, generate the PDF, mark it ready, record its submission to the manufacturer. The manufacturer prints the cards and mails them to customers directly. Renda does NOT track shipping or delivery, and the admin must never show a delivery state, tracking reference or shipping status. The lifecycle after submission is driven by one signal: the customer activating the card (reported by the card processor; `recordCardActivation` in the operations service). Activation moves the card to Active, completes the order as "Activated", and retires any card it replaces. A card still In Production `ACTIVATION_FOLLOWUP_DAYS` (14) after submission surfaces in the attention queue as "Card not activated", and a submitted, never-activated order can still be canceled so a replacement can be issued.

## Plans and billing (product rule, user, 2026-11-17)

Plus is charged from the customer's Smart Spend account. An upgrade charges the fee immediately, in the same action (`changePlan` in the operations service): if Smart Spend cannot cover it, the upgrade does not happen and the customer stays on Everyday. There is no pending upgrade state. A failed monthly renewal does not suspend the plan and is never a "Billing Issue" status (that status does not exist): the fee becomes outstanding (`billingOutstandingSince`), the plan stays active, and the charge is retried at intervals (`planRetryIntervalDays` in Settings; the exact interval is still being decided) for the rest of the billing month, including automatic collection whenever funds land. Retries are recorded as billing-system events (`recordRenewalAttempt`), not employee simulations of a pending charge. If the fee is still unpaid when the new billing date arrives (the subscription's `nextBillingDate`, advanced past the missed date at the first failure; `billingDowngradeDue` in `src/services/rules.ts`), the plan is downgraded to Everyday (`downgradeForNonPayment`), which also cancels physical cards and open card orders because Everyday is virtual-only. Physical cards exist only for Plus members, and account issuance always creates Smart Spend and Sense Save together.

## Contact details and verification (product rule, user, 2026-11-17)

Email is verified at sign-up (proof of humanity) and phone during onboarding/KYC (bank requirement). After that, contact details can change without re-verification or re-KYC: customers update them in the app with OTP, and support can update them after the customer passes identity verification on the call or chat, in which case the verified flags stay set (`updateCustomer` must never clear them). The sign-up email is kept permanently on the customer record (`signupEmail`) alongside the current email, and every change is in the audit log with before/after values.

## Transfers and transaction finality (product rule, user, 2026-11-17)

Transactions are never reversed by employees; the remedy for a bad transaction is an investigation case. RenPay follows the Revolut model for internal transfers: payments are instant and cannot be cancelled or reversed once completed. Renda can ask the recipient to return the money on the sender's behalf (`requestTransferReturn`); funds move only with the recipient's consent, and a return is recorded as a new transfer back (`recordReturnOutcome`), never as an edit to the original. The one real cancellation is Interac: a transfer that is still Pending (not yet accepted or deposited) can be cancelled (`cancelInteracTransfer`), by the customer in the app or by an admin here. Completed Interac transfers are final.

## Support cases and Alfie events (product rule, user, 2026-11-17)

Customer conversations and ticketing live on a separate support platform, not in this admin. Cases here are internal work orders: a supervisor creates one when an interaction needs backend work or a follow-up inside Renda's systems, and assigns it to whoever should do the work. Notes are internal by design and there is deliberately no customer reply channel. Alfie operational events are resolved with a mandatory resolution note plus who/when (`resolveAlfieEvent`, permission `alfie.manage`), and can be reopened if the issue recurs.

## Restrictions and closure (product rule, user, 2026-11-17)

Two kinds of restriction: an operational freeze (Support or Banking Operations; suspected fraud, stolen phone, customer request) and a compliance hold (Compliance only; AML review, sanctions). A freeze can be escalated to a hold. Restriction blocks outbound activity only: the customer can log in and receive money but cannot send, spend or withdraw. Cards are deliberately NOT blocked or frozen by a restriction: they stop working because spending is outbound, whatever their own status says. Only Compliance can lift a restriction from the admin (`customers.hold`); the backend also carries a simple restrict true/false flag. Every set and lift takes a reason from the fixed list plus a mandatory note and is audit-logged. A restriction can be placed from Active or from Setup In Progress (accounts are issued and live in both; earlier stages have nothing outbound to block), and lifting restores the status the customer had when the restriction was placed (`previousStatus` on the restriction record), so a customer frozen during setup returns to Setup In Progress, never straight to Active. Closure is customer-requested or bank-initiated (inactivity, abuse, failed KYC). Closing deactivates the customer's Renda account itself, not just their banking accounts, so it is available at EVERY lifecycle stage, including a customer who only just registered and has no accounts or balance yet. Before closing, any remaining balance is paid out by Interac e-Transfer to an Interac email the customer chose or the one on their account; savings goals close and their funds are part of the Sense Save payout; the Plus subscription is cancelled with no refund; every card becomes inactive. A customer can also ask to close from the app: the request lands on their record (`closureRequest`) with the exit reason they picked, their feedback, the Interac email they chose, and a balance snapshot from when they asked. Closure requests have their own section in the admin (Closure Requests in the sidebar; selecting a request in that list opens the request itself, never the customer profile first, and the profile is reached only through the explicit Open full profile action), and processing there is stepped, never a single button: step 1 assign (`assignClosureRequest` moves the request to In Progress with an assignee, themselves or another employee; nothing is paid at this step), step 2 the payout (live balances, the payout email, correctable with the correction recorded, and the method), step 3 complete (`processClosureRequest`). The method is Automated (the system sends the Interac payout as part of the closure) or Manual (the assignee sent the payout outside the admin and records its Interac reference, which the closure adopts instead of creating a second payout; a reference is mandatory for Manual). When the live balance is zero there is no payout at all: the method is None, step 2 has nothing to prepare, step 3 only confirms the closure, and the service rejects None while any balance remains payable. Completion is one-time and cannot duplicate: the request must be Pending or In Progress, processing marks it Processed inside the closure, a direct admin close also fulfils an open request, and a closed customer cannot be closed again. An open request can instead be cancelled without closing (`cancelClosureRequest`, mandatory note), for example when the customer changes their mind. A closed customer can be reactivated by Compliance on appeal only inside a 90-day window (accounts reopen at zero; cards and subscription are not restored); after that closure is permanent and a returning customer registers fresh, reusing the same email and phone with full KYC (the Revolut model). RenPay return requests exist in the service layer but the admin button is disabled until the customer app has the recipient-side RenPay screens.

## UI architecture

- Use the shared semantic tokens, Button, PageHeader, Panel and table controls for every employee module; visual refinements must not modify service rules or data relationships.
- Keep customer overview sections in the shared unboxed summary workspace and preserve URL-backed profile tabs, so detailed records remain accessible without fragmented containers.
- Use SelectField to restyle existing select fields while retaining their option values and event handlers; shared dropdown presentation prevents inconsistent native controls.

## Employee access

- Every dashboard page lives under `src/routes/_authenticated/`, whose gate (`resolveAccess` in `src/services/directory.ts`) requires an Active employee with a role, completed MFA when enrolled, and a non-temporary password; this keeps sign-in, MFA and first-password rules in one place.
- All backend access goes through the contracts in `src/services/contracts/` (`AuthService`, `EmployeeAdminApi`, `OperationsService`, `OperationsRepository`), wired in one place, `src/services/api.ts`; pages never import an implementation, so a real backend replaces the in-browser mocks in `src/services/mock/` without UI changes.
- Employee writes must check the caller's permission and the rules in `employee-rules.ts` inside the service implementation (the mock does this in `src/services/mock/employees.ts`), so business rules (last Administrator, self-changes, automatic Invitation Pending) can't be bypassed from the UI.
- Pure access rules live in `src/services/employee-rules.ts` with tests in `src/test/employee-rules.test.ts`; mock-service behavior is covered by `src/test/mock-backend.test.ts`.
- `docs/schema.sql` is the reference schema for employee tables and must stay in step with `src/services/contracts/employees.ts`.
- The operations dataset is a synchronous React snapshot (`useDB`) loaded through `OperationsRepository` by the workspace gate (`loadOperations`); writes before it loads are never persisted, so stale state can't overwrite saved records.
- The demo operations dataset reads permissions through the live role registry (`setRolePermissions`) and mirrors directory employees into the local store, so custom roles apply to every existing module without changing module code.
- Temporary testing mode (user decision, 2026-11-15): `OPEN_SIGN_IN` is ON in `src/services/mock/auth.ts`. Any credentials that do not match a real employee sign in as the ephemeral Test Administrator (`ensureTestAdmin` in `src/services/mock/backend.ts`), a fixed generic identity that lives only in memory: the typed credentials are never stored or turned into an account, the identity is stripped from every persisted tables write, its session is memory-only (a reload signs it out), it leaves no audit or last-login record, and it does not count as a safeguard Administrator. Real accounts sign in normally and take precedence. While the flag is on, the first-run setup screen is skipped (`needsInitialSetup` returns false) and failed-attempt rate limiting is bypassed. Turn `OPEN_SIGN_IN` off before any real use: strict mode has no shared or fallback account, sign-in then offers the one-time first-administrator setup (`needsInitialSetup` / `setupInitialAdmin` in the AuthService contract) only while no real accounts exist, and failed sign-ins are rate-limited per email, returning the contract's "rate-limited" error.
